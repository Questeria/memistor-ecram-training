\begin{abstract}
In 1960 Widrow and Hoff stored the weights of their adaptive neuron in memistors: three-terminal electrochemical cells whose
conductance is set by the charge through a third terminal. Their modern descendants are lithium-ion, metal-oxide and protonic
ECRAM. We ask, in simulation, whether such devices can train neural networks in memory to near-digital accuracy, what
specifications they need, and what energy advantage survives once the periphery is counted.

\textbf{Simulator and validation.} Our device model includes levels, asymmetry, saturation, write noise, spreads, drift and
reference error. Every check is shown to fail on a planted fault. We validate it against a published analog-training result:
for the RPU-baseline device on MNIST we obtain \REOneFpErr\%, \REOneSgdErr\% and \REOneTtvOneErr\% test error for the exact update,
SGD and Tiki-Taka. The published values are 2.0\%, about 15\% and about 2\%. We also reproduce the published robustness of
chopped TTv2 and AGAD to reference offsets.

\textbf{Main comparison.} In a pre-registered comparison with five seeds per arm, we test four published device specifications
and Widrow's memistor on MNIST, a CIFAR-10 subset and a character language model on human-written text.
\begin{itemize}
\item Plain SGD fails on every published device and task.
\item With a Tiki-Taka-family algorithm, the Li-ECRAM and protonic specifications reach the baseline within our gap lines or
      within five-seed resolution on all three tasks.
\item The best language-model gap is \REThreeLmBestGap\% [\REThreeLmBestLo, \REThreeLmBestHi].
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Scale.} When every arm tunes its own learning rate, the gap does not grow with width; an earlier observation of growth
was a learning-rate artefact.

\textbf{Specification sheet.} Sensitivity sweeps show that the binding specifications are an accurate symmetry-point reference
($\sigma_r$ \RSpecrelTtvTwoCifarWThreeTwoRefErr{} of the half-range for TTv2) and a saturating response. A few hundred levels suffice.

\textbf{Energy.} With periphery, at the efficiency of a measured analog-AI chip, training energy per weight is about
\REnRatioMin$\times$ below an H100 at its measured utilisation, \REnRangeLo--\REnRangeHi$\times$ over our sensitivity range. The
read periphery, not the femtojoule device, dominates.

We release the simulator, the records, a device-experiment roadmap with pass criteria, and a bench replication of the 1960
memistor.
\end{abstract}
